Title: Bid-Ask Spread
Source: FINRA
URL: https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/bid-ask-spread
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
The bid-ask spread is the difference between the highest price a buyer is willing to pay (the bid) and the lowest price a seller is willing to accept (the ask). The spread represents an implicit cost of trading.

## Key points
- **Bid vs ask:** When you buy, you usually pay the ask price. When you sell, you receive the bid price.
- **Hidden transaction cost:** The difference between bid and ask is a cost that reduces your effective return.
- **Liquidity indicator:** Narrow (tight) spreads generally indicate high liquidity and heavy trading volume. Wide spreads are more common in thinly traded or volatile securities.
- **Market makers:** In many markets, market makers help provide liquidity and may earn compensation through the spread.
- **ETFs and stocks:** Large, heavily traded ETFs and blue-chip stocks often have very tight spreads. Small-cap stocks or niche ETFs may have wider spreads.

## Common misconceptions
- **"The last traded price is what I will get."** Not necessarily. You typically transact at the current bid or ask, not the last trade price.
- **"The spread doesn’t matter for long-term investors."** Even small spreads can add up if you trade frequently or invest in illiquid securities.

## Example
If a stock shows a bid of $99.90 and an ask of $100.10, the spread is $0.20. If you place a market order to buy, you will likely pay $100.10. If you immediately sold, you would likely receive $99.90—resulting in a $0.20 difference before considering any commissions.
