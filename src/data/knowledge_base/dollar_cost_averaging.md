Title: Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA)

AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
Dollar cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals (for example, monthly) regardless of market conditions. Over time, you buy more shares when prices are low and fewer shares when prices are high.

## Key points
- **Reduces timing risk:** You avoid investing all your money at once at a market peak.
- **Encourages discipline:** Regular investing can reduce emotional, reactive decisions.
- **Common in retirement plans:** Many 401(k) contributions are effectively DCA since investments occur each paycheck.
- **Not always higher returns:** Historically, lump-sum investing has often produced higher returns because markets tend to rise over time—but it comes with more short-term timing risk.

## Common misconceptions
- **“DCA guarantees profits.”** No. It does not protect against losses in a declining market.
- **“DCA always beats lump sum investing.”** Not necessarily. If markets rise steadily, investing earlier can result in higher returns.

## Example
If you invest $500 every month into a broad market ETF, you will automatically buy more shares when prices dip and fewer shares when prices rise. Over time, this can smooth out the average purchase price, though it does not eliminate market risk.
