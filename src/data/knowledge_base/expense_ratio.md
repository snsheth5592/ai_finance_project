Title: Expense Ratios
Source: FINRA
URL: https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/fund-fees
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
A fund’s expense ratio is the annual fee charged by the fund, expressed as a percentage of the assets you invest. Even small differences in expense ratios can meaningfully affect long-term returns.

## Key points
- **Annual percentage fee:** If a fund has a 0.50% expense ratio, you pay $5 per year for every $1,000 invested (before market gains or losses).
- **Automatically deducted:** The fee is typically taken out of the fund’s assets, so you don’t receive a separate bill.
- **Compounding impact:** Higher fees reduce the amount of money that can grow over time.
- **Compare before investing:** When comparing similar funds, lower expense ratios can be an advantage—if other factors are similar.

## Common misconceptions
- **“Expense ratios are a one-time fee.”** No. They are charged every year.
- **“A higher fee always means better performance.”** Not necessarily. Some higher-cost funds underperform lower-cost alternatives.

## Example
If you invest $10,000 in a fund with a 1.00% expense ratio, about $100 per year is deducted from the fund’s assets. Over decades, that difference can compound and significantly reduce your final balance compared to a lower-cost fund.
