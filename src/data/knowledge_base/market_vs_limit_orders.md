Title: Market vs Limit Orders
Source: FINRA
URL: https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/market-vs-limit-orders
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
A market order executes immediately at the best available price. A limit order executes only at your specified price (or better), but may not fill if the market never reaches that price.

## Key points
- **Market order:** Prioritizes speed of execution over exact price.
- **Limit order:** Prioritizes price control over guaranteed execution.
- **Slippage risk:** In volatile or thinly traded markets, market orders may execute at prices worse than expected.
- **Liquidity matters:** Highly liquid securities typically have tighter bid-ask spreads, reducing execution risk.
- **Order expiration:** Limit orders can be day orders (expire at end of trading day) or good-til-canceled (GTC), depending on brokerage settings.

## Common misconceptions
- **"Market orders always get the displayed price."** Not necessarily. The execution price can differ from the last quoted price, especially in fast-moving markets.
- **"Limit orders always execute if the price touches the limit."** Not guaranteed. There must be sufficient trading volume available at that price.

## Example
If a stock is trading at $100 and you place a market order to buy, your order executes at the next available price, which might be slightly above $100. If you instead place a limit order at $99, your order will only execute if the market price drops to $99 or lower.
