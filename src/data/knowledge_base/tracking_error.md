Title: Tracking Error
Source: Investopedia (Educational Overview)
URL: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/trackingerror.asp
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
Tracking error measures how closely an index fund or ETF follows its benchmark index. A low tracking error means the fund’s returns closely match the index; a higher tracking error means the returns diverge more significantly from the benchmark.

## Key points
- **What it measures:** The difference between a fund’s returns and the returns of the index it is designed to track.
- **Common causes:** Expense ratios, partial replication (holding only a sample of the index), cash holdings, trading costs, and rebalancing timing.
- **Expense ratio effect:** Because fees reduce returns, a fund with a 0.10% expense ratio will typically underperform its benchmark by roughly that amount annually, all else equal.
- **Volatility of differences:** Tracking error reflects the variability of return differences over time—not just a one-year gap.

## Common misconceptions
- **“Tracking error means the fund is poorly managed.”** Not necessarily. Some tracking error is normal due to fees and operational factors.
- **“A fund with slightly higher tracking error is always worse.”** Not always. Differences may be temporary or related to market conditions.

## Example
If an index returns 8% in a year and an ETF tracking that index returns 7.9%, the 0.1% difference may largely reflect the fund’s expense ratio and operational costs. Over many years, small differences like this can compound and slightly affect total returns.
