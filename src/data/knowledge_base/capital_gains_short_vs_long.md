Title: Capital Gains: Short-Term vs Long-Term
Source: IRS.gov
URL: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
Capital gains are profits from selling an investment for more than you paid. The tax rate depends on how long you held the investment before selling it. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income, while long-term gains generally receive lower tax rates.

## Key points
- **Short-term capital gains:** Assets held for one year or less are taxed at ordinary income tax rates (which can be higher).
- **Long-term capital gains:** Assets held for more than one year are typically taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% federally, depending on taxable income (plus potential Net Investment Income Tax for higher earners).
- **Holding period rule:** The holding period begins the day after purchase and includes the day of sale.
- **Tax impact:** Even a one-day difference beyond one year can change a gain from short-term to long-term tax treatment.
- **Wash sale rule:** If you sell an investment at a loss and repurchase the same or substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss may be disallowed for tax purposes.

## Common misconceptions
- **"All investment gains are taxed the same."** No. Tax treatment depends on holding period and account type.
- **"Long-term always means one calendar year."** Not exactly. It must be more than one year (for example, 366 days if not a leap year edge case).
- **"Taxes are only due when you withdraw money."** In taxable brokerage accounts, selling an investment can trigger capital gains tax even if you reinvest the proceeds.

## Example
If you buy shares for $5,000 and sell them 10 months later for $6,000, the $1,000 gain is short-term and taxed at your ordinary income rate. If you instead sell after holding the shares for more than one year, that $1,000 gain would typically qualify for long-term capital gains tax rates, which may be lower.
