## Summary
An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a fund that holds a basket of assets (such as stocks or bonds) and trades on an exchange like a stock. Many ETFs are designed to track an index, sector, commodity, or a rules-based strategy.

## Key points
- **Basket exposure:** One ETF share can give you exposure to many underlying holdings.
- **Trades like a stock:** ETFs generally trade throughout the trading day (prices can move while markets are open).
- **Costs matter:** ETFs often have an **expense ratio** (an annual fee expressed as a percentage).
- **Different types exist:** Broad-market index ETFs, bond ETFs, sector ETFs, thematic ETFs, and more.

## Common misconceptions
- **“ETFs are always safer than stocks.”** Not necessarily. Risk depends on what the ETF holds (e.g., a broad index ETF vs a leveraged sector ETF).
- **“All ETFs are the same.”** No. Holdings, fees, liquidity, and strategy can differ a lot.

## Example
Instead of buying 50 different stocks one by one, you could buy one broad-market ETF that holds those stocks—still subject to market ups and downs, but with built-in diversification across many companies.
