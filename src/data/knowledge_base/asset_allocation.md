## Summary
Asset allocation is how you divide your investments among different asset classes—such as stocks, bonds, and cash—to balance risk and return based on your goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

## Key points
- **Risk vs return tradeoff:** Stocks historically offer higher potential returns but greater volatility. Bonds typically offer lower returns with less volatility. Cash provides stability but limited growth.
- **Time horizon matters:** Longer investment horizons may allow greater exposure to stocks, while shorter horizons often emphasize bonds and cash.
- **Diversification across asset classes:** Combining assets that behave differently can reduce overall portfolio volatility.
- **Rebalancing is part of allocation:** Over time, market movements change portfolio weights. Periodic rebalancing helps maintain the intended risk profile.
- **Individual factors:** Age, income stability, financial goals, and comfort with volatility influence allocation decisions.

## Common misconceptions
- **"There is one best allocation for everyone."** No. Asset allocation depends on personal circumstances and objectives.
- **"More stocks always means better long-term results."** Not necessarily. Higher stock exposure increases both potential return and potential losses.
- **"Asset allocation guarantees protection from losses."** No allocation eliminates market risk.

## Example
A long-term investor saving for retirement might allocate a larger percentage to stocks to pursue growth, while an investor approaching a near-term goal (such as buying a home) might allocate more to bonds and cash to reduce short-term volatility. These examples are illustrative and not personalized recommendations.
