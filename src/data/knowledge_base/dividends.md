Title: Dividends
Source: SEC Investor.gov
URL: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks

## Summary
Dividends are payments that some companies make to shareholders from their profits. They can provide income and, when reinvested, contribute to long-term growth.

## Key points
- **Types:** Qualified dividends are taxed at long-term capital gains rates; nonqualified (ordinary) dividends are taxed as ordinary income.
- **Dividend yield:** Annual dividend per share divided by share price. High yield isn't always better—it can signal a struggling company or a dividend cut risk.
- **Reinvestment:** Dividend reinvestment plans (DRIPs) or brokerage auto-reinvest can compound growth by buying more shares with dividends.
- **Not guaranteed:** Companies can cut or suspend dividends, especially in downturns.

## ETF and fund dividends
ETFs and mutual funds that hold dividend-paying stocks pass through dividends to investors, typically on a quarterly or monthly schedule.

## Summary
Dividends are payments that some companies make to shareholders from their profits. They can provide income and, when reinvested, contribute to long-term growth through compounding.

## Key points
- **Income component:** Dividends represent a share of company profits distributed to shareholders.
- **Types of dividends:** Qualified dividends are generally taxed at long-term capital gains rates, while nonqualified (ordinary) dividends are taxed as regular income.
- **Dividend yield:** Calculated as annual dividend per share divided by share price. A very high yield can sometimes indicate higher risk.
- **Reinvestment:** Dividend reinvestment plans (DRIPs) automatically use dividends to purchase additional shares.
- **Not guaranteed:** Companies may reduce or suspend dividends during financial stress.

## ETF and fund dividends
ETFs and mutual funds that hold dividend-paying stocks typically pass dividends through to investors. The timing (monthly, quarterly, etc.) depends on the fund.

## Common misconceptions
- **"Dividends are free money."** No. When a dividend is paid, the stock price typically adjusts downward by roughly the dividend amount.
- **"High dividend stocks are always safer."** Not necessarily. A high yield may reflect higher business risk or expectations of a dividend cut.

## Example
If you own 100 shares of a company that pays a $1 annual dividend per share, you would receive $100 per year (before taxes). If you reinvest those dividends, you buy more shares, which may generate additional dividends in the future.
