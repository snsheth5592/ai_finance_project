Title: ETF vs Mutual Fund
Source: SEC Investor.gov
URL: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/etfs
AsOf: 2026-02-15

## Summary
ETFs (exchange-traded funds) and mutual funds both pool investor money to hold a diversified basket of securities. However, they differ in how they trade, how they are priced, and often in their fee structures and tax efficiency.

## Key points
- **Trading mechanics:** ETFs trade on an exchange throughout the day like stocks. Mutual funds are bought or sold once per day at their net asset value (NAV), calculated after market close.
- **Pricing behavior:** ETF prices can trade slightly above or below the value of their underlying holdings (called a premium or discount). Mutual funds always transact at NAV.
- **Minimum investment:** Many mutual funds require a minimum initial investment. ETFs can typically be purchased in single-share increments (depending on brokerage policies).
- **Fees:** Both charge expense ratios, but ETFs often have lower average expense ratios. Some mutual funds may charge sales loads (front-end or back-end fees).
- **Tax efficiency:** ETFs are often more tax-efficient because of their “in-kind” creation and redemption process, which can reduce capital gains distributions.

## Common misconceptions
- **“ETFs are always better than mutual funds.”** Not necessarily. The better choice depends on investor needs, such as automatic investing features or intraday trading flexibility.
- **“Mutual funds are outdated.”** Many mutual funds are actively managed and may provide different strategies than index-tracking ETFs.

## Example
If you want to invest in a broad U.S. stock index, you could choose either an ETF that tracks the index and trades during the day, or a mutual fund version that processes purchases at the end-of-day NAV. Both provide similar market exposure, but the trading mechanics and tax treatment may differ.
